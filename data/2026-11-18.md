Abstract: Health researchers commonly use weighting methods to improve covariate balance when estimating program and intervention effects from observational data. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) and variance ratios assess particular features of balance but may overlook residual differences elsewhere in the covariate distribution. This paper provides practical guidance for using Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS), Anderson-Darling (AD), and Cramer-von Mises (CVM) statistics as complementary distributional balance diagnostics. In an empirical evaluation of a disease management program, weighting based on a main-effects propensity-score model produced SMD and KS statistics below commonly used or proposed balance thresholds of 0.10 for two continuous covariates, which would conventionally be interpreted as indicating acceptable balance. Nevertheless, permutation tests identified residual distributional differences. Adding quadratic terms for these covariates approximately halved their KS statistics, improved variance balance, and eliminated evidence of residual distributional differences, while leaving the estimated program effect substantively unchanged. Supporting simulations showed that KS, AD, and CVM differed in sensitivity to different forms of distributional discrepancy and that the calibration of resampling-based p-values depended on how the reference distribution was constructed. These findings support a practical approach that combines conventional balance measures, graphical assessment, complementary distributional diagnostics, weighting-model refinement, and outcome adjustment when important residual imbalance remains.
