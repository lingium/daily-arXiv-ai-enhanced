Method: 将每个观测在CV估计中视为"评估角色"与"训练角色"的双重加权影响，权重由随机划分决定；由此推导出CV估计方差的精确有限样本恒等式，其常数只依赖于模型、度量与总体，适用于对观测平均的指标（如MSE）和对病例–对照对平均的指标（如AUC）；进一步做一阶方差分析与渐近正态性证明。

Result: 方差可分解为抽样方差（仅取决于训练集大小）与划分随机性方差（当不同划分下权重变化时产生）；权重按设计在各观测间相等的K折在一阶意义下方差最优，Monte Carlo与bootstrap CV不是；K折的一阶方差与K无关，且在模型合理条件下留一法在所有K折中方差最小；对AUC，对应的leave-pair-out具有同样性质；CV估计渐近正态，且其渐近方差即恒等式给出的完整方差。

Conclusion: 该工作为交叉验证性能的统计推断提供了通用理论基础，并给出了选择方差最优CV设计的原则：优先使用权重均衡的K折，追求最小方差时可取留一法（AUC取leave-pair-out）。

Abstract: Cross-validation estimates predictive performance by repeatedly splitting the data into training and validation sets, fitting the model on the first and evaluating it on the second. Every observation influences the estimate in two roles, through its own evaluation when it is held out and through the models it helps fit when it trains. The random splits determine its weight in each role. From this representation, we prove an exact finite-sample identity for the variance of the cross-validated estimate. For a given splitting scheme with a common training set size, the variance is a function of these weights, with constants of the model, the metric, and the population. It covers metrics averaged over observations, such as mean squared error, and over case--control pairs, such as the AUC. The identity shows which schemes to use. The variance decomposes into sampling variance, which depends only on the training size, and variance due to the random fold splitting, which arises when the weights change from one draw of the splits to the next. K-fold cross-validation, whose weights are equal across observations by design, is therefore variance optimal at first order; Monte Carlo and bootstrap cross-validation are not. The first-order variance of K-fold does not depend on K, and under reasonable conditions on the model leave-one-out has the smallest variance of any K-fold scheme. For the AUC the same holds for leave-pair-out, which evaluates every case--control pair. Finally, we show the cross-validated estimate is asymptotically normal with the identity's full variance. Together, these results provide a general theoretical foundation for inference on cross-validated performance and establish principles for selecting variance-optimal cross-validation designs.
